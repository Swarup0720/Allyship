About the Initiative

Why “Allyship++”?

Allyship is the deliberate, ongoing practice in which leaders with institutional power work in active solidarity with emerging and underrepresented talent. The “++” signifies continuous evolution—incrementing inclusion day after day.

The Genesis of Allyship++

Industry and Academia, in the Same Room

For decades, university placement in India operated as a yearly, one-way transaction: institutions waited on campus for recruiters to arrive. Allyship++ reverses that equation. Siksha ‘O’ Anusandhan travels directly to the corporate boardrooms of Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Mumbai, and Chennai to listen first. While a diversity policy states what an organisation intends on paper, allyship determines what happens on an ordinary Tuesday—who receives credit in a meeting, who is sponsored for a stretch project, and who speaks up when a boundary is crossed.

The “++” carries a dual meaning rooted in engineering and organizational leadership: in programming, the ++ operator increments a value by one step at a time, and marks the historic leap from procedural C to object-oriented C++. Applied to corporate-academic leadership, Allyship++ represents a continuous, compounding upgrade—expanding traditional DEI to encompass generational allyship for Gen-Z professionals, psychological safety for junior analysts, and co-created curricula between universities and employers.

Uniquely, every Allyship++ chapter pairs corporate HR and technology panels with India’s sporting legends—from Olympic gold medalist Gurbux Singh and athletics pioneer Anju Bobby George to Test cricketers Arshad Ayub and Nilesh Kulkarni, and Paris Olympics sprint coach Premanand J.—proving that the mentorship, discipline, and trust that win medals in the arena are identical to the allyship that builds high-performing teams in the boardroom.

Behavioral Framework & Governance

From Policy to Practice: The 7 Roles of Workplace Allyship

Grounded in Karen Catlin’s Better Allies framework and insights from 40+ CXO panelists across SOA’s conclaves.

1. Sponsor

Institutional Clout

Opening doors behind closed doors—recommending emerging talent for stretch projects, panels, and promotions with concrete performance evidence.

2. Champion

Public Advocacy

Publicly endorsing junior colleagues and Gen-Z professionals in high-visibility leadership forums, town halls, and client reviews.

3. Amplifier

Credit Attribution

Crediting ideas out loud—when a colleague's idea is overlooked in a meeting, bringing it back to the table and explicitly naming who originated it.

4. Advocate

Structural Reform

Using positional influence to widen access, audit hiring and performance evaluation rubrics, and remove systemic bias.

5. Scholar

Continuous Learning

Doing independent learning on workplace barriers—listening first in ERGs so junior or underrepresented colleagues do not bear the teaching burden.

6. Upstander

Real-Time Intervention

Speaking up calmly in the moment when a comment or action crosses a line, backed by institutional safety, and checking in privately afterward.

7. Confidante

Psychological Safety

Building team cultures where colleagues feel safe to voice concerns, admit knowledge gaps, and seek mentorship without retaliation fear.

8. 3-Question Test

Authenticity Litmus Test

1) Does it cost the leader comfort or capital? 2) Does it tangibly alter career outcomes? 3) Would it still happen behind closed doors?
